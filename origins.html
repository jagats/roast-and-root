Coffee Origins

Four Regions. One Journey.

India grows nearly all of its coffee in the southern hill ranges, under forest shade. These are the four regions we source from — and why.

The Route

A Journey Across India's Coffee Hills

Every buying season, our green coffee buyer travels south through four distinct landscapes. Follow the route below, then meet each region in turn.

  1. Araku Valley

    First stop: the Eastern Ghats of Andhra Pradesh, where tribal-run farms perch between 900 and 1,300 metres.

  2. Chikmagalur

    West into Karnataka's Baba Budangiri hills — the birthplace of Indian coffee, and home of our oldest estate partner.

  3. Coorg

    South to Kodagu, the largest coffee-growing district in India, where coffee shares the canopy with cardamom and pepper.

  4. Nilgiri Hills

    The final climb: the Blue Mountains of Tamil Nadu, home to our highest — and slowest-ripening — lots.

Stop 01 · Andhra Pradesh

Araku Valley

Araku is a high, folded valley in the Eastern Ghats where coffee was introduced to tribal communities in the 1950s and has since transformed hillside livelihoods. Farms here are tiny — one to three hectares — and sit on terraces between 900 and 1,300 metres, shaded by silver oak and mango trees.

Growing conditions

Red laterite soils, cool nights, and a well-defined monsoon followed by a dry ripening season — conditions that produce dense, sweet cherries.

Coffee characteristics & tasting notes

Araku coffees are aromatic and bright: jasmine, citrus and caramel are the recurring signatures, with a light tea-like body in washed lots.

Why we source here

We buy through a smallholder collective that certifies its own picking and drying standards, paying a fixed premium agreed before harvest. The pride growers take in their lots shows up unmistakably in the cup.

Stop 02 · Karnataka

Chikmagalur

Chikmagalur is where Indian coffee began: legend has it that Baba Budan carried seven coffee seeds from Yemen to these hills in the 17th century. Estates here have been planted for generations, their red-earth lanes winding between towering silver oaks and century-old shade canopies.

Growing conditions

Elevations of 1,000–1,500 metres, deep well-drained forest soil, and steady rainfall — textbook conditions that make Chikmagalur reliably excellent year after year.

Coffee characteristics & tasting notes

Expect clean washed coffees with crisp acidity: jasmine, lemon zest and honey are the flavours we chase in our Chikmagalur lots.

Why we source here

Our partner estate has recorded every harvest since 1904. That depth of knowledge — and their willingness to experiment with new processing for us — makes Chikmagalur our most dependable origin.

Stop 03 · Karnataka

Coorg

Kodagu — Coorg — is India's coffee heartland, producing more coffee than any other district in the country. Coffee grows here beneath a layered canopy of spice vines, cardamom plants and pepper-climbed shade trees, which perfume the air and, locals will tell you, the coffee too.

Growing conditions

Heavy monsoon rainfall (up to 3,000 mm a year), humid air, and elevations of 900–1,400 metres. The monsoon slows drying and concentrates sugars — a challenge the best growers turn into an advantage.

Coffee characteristics & tasting notes

Coorg coffees are full-bodied and deeply sweet: dark chocolate, brown sugar and a gentle cardamom warmth. Our natural-processed Coorg lots add strawberry and winey fruit.

Why we source here

Monsoon-grown Coorg coffee gives our darker roasts their backbone. We work with an estate that composts all processing water and sends our roaster chaff back as mulch — a genuine closed loop.

Stop 04 · Tamil Nadu

Nilgiri Hills

The Nilgiris — the Blue Mountains — rise to nearly 1,800 metres, the highest coffee we buy. Here cherries ripen weeks later than anywhere else on our route, accumulating sugars slowly in cool mountain air that often carries a morning chill close to frost.

Growing conditions

High elevations of 1,200–1,800 metres, cool nights, and a dry, sunny ripening season on steep slopes. Slow maturation means lower yields — and remarkable clarity.

Coffee characteristics & tasting notes

High-grown Nilgiri coffee is delicate and perfumed: white peach, black tea and cane sugar, with a silky body in honey-processed lots.

Why we source here

A cluster of smallholder families on a Kotagiri ridge picks and sorts with extraordinary care. Their summit plots give us our rarest releases — barely fifty kilos a harvest.
